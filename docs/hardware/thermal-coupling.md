Cooling rejects heat beneath each target. The shared aluminium carrier can conduct this heat into the background and neighbouring targets.

<div class="equation">Heat rejected = heat removed from the target + electrical input</div>

## Heat flow

The heat sink must dissipate heat removed from the target plus electrical input. Rising sink temperature reduces cooling performance.

![Calculated relationship between heat removed, electrical input and rejected heat](../assets/setup/heat-balance.svg)

Calculated energy balance; not experimental data.

## Proposed heat-sink assembly

1. Mount a **separate finned aluminium heat sink** against the hot underside of each target.
2. Use a thin thermal interface and even clamping to maintain contact.
3. Place the fan below the arena and direct air through the fins. Route the warm exhaust away from the carrier and outside the fly chamber.

<figure class="photo-slot" data-photo-id="underside-airflow"><div class="photo-space" aria-label="Blank photograph of the underside heat sinks and fan"></div><figcaption><span>Photo · H10</span><strong>Underside and airflow path</strong></figcaption></figure>

Provide clear intake and exhaust paths below the arena. Keep airflow outside the walking chamber.

## Target isolation

Target openings reduce conduction into the carrier. Maintain a flush, fly-tight floor without metal bridges across the thermal gaps.

Machine openings to measured module dimensions, with adequate clamping and support.

## Validation

Compare target, background and underside temperature traces at matched initial conditions, target order and drive settings.
